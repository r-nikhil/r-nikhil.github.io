*89 examples of AI, and the people steering it, doing ambitious things quickly.*

Patrick Collison keeps a list called [Fast](https://patrickcollison.com/fast), about ambitious things that got done quickly. I wrote the other half of it a few weeks ago in [Unglamorous](/2026/11/07/unglamorous). This is the AI edition, put together at [@rauchg's request](https://x.com/rauchg/status/2124386118661738994).

The rules: one entry per feat, with what happened, how long it took (⏱), who was in the loop (👥) and a primary source. Lab and company claims are labeled, and estimates are flagged.

---

### Math

- **Riemann Hypothesis.** Levent Alpöge and Claude went from first idea to a full Lean proof in about 41 days (Oct 6 – Nov 16, 2026). The ~17M lines of Lean were released on Nov 20, and the Lean FRO and Mathlib Initiative certified them a day later. *(Open since 1859. The timeline is a rough one that Claude reconstructed for Alpöge's own note. The Lean proof is checked, but a human-readable proof is still being written.)*<br><small>⏱ ~41 days · 👥 1 mathematician + Claude · [Lean community](https://leanprover-community.github.io/blog/posts/riemann-hypothesis/) · [Alpöge](https://alpo.ge/remark.pdf) · [Independent checks (Sutherland)](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/)</small>
- **Navier–Stokes (Millennium Prize).** About 10,000 OpenAI agents, steered by its researchers, reached a blow-up result 88 hours after launch. GPT-6 Astra then formalized it in Lean in 17 more hours. *(OpenAI's own claim, for flow with a smooth forcing term. A later preprint found mismatches between the written proof and the Lean version, expert review is still going, and credit is disputed.)*<br><small>⏱ 88 h + 17 h · 👥 OpenAI researchers + ~10,000 agents · [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) · [Clay](https://www.claymath.org/news/navier-stokes-announcement/) · [Critique (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) · [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/09/22/nx-s1-5968588/openai-navier-stokes-problem-mathematicians-learn-little)</small>
- **Jacobian conjecture, disproved.** Alpöge posted a short 3-D counterexample that Claude Fable found while the 2026 World Cup final was being played (Jul 19). Terence Tao published a digestion of it on Jul 21. *(Posed by Keller in 1939. The two-variable case is still open. No source gives the total working time.)*<br><small>⏱ During one match (Alpöge's account) · 👥 1 mathematician + Claude · [Alpöge on X](https://x.com/__alpoge__/status/2079028340955197566) · [Terence Tao](https://terrytao.wordpress.com/2026/07/21/a-digestion-of-the-jacobian-conjecture-counterexample/) · [Isabelle check](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Jacobian_Counterexample.html)</small>
- **Fermat's Last Theorem in Lean.** Claude agents wrote the first end-to-end computer-checked proof of FLT in 11 days: ~13M lines of Lean and ~29,500 intermediate theorems. Later, 1,000 Fable 5.2 agents redid it in about 30 hours, starting from 60 target statements and 47 lemmas taken from the first proof. *(Anthropic's account; Kevin Buzzard compiled and checked the first proof. The human Lean project was under way by 2023 and is funded for 2024–2029.)*<br><small>⏱ 11 days, then ~30 h · 👥 1 researcher guiding agents / 1,000 agents · [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/formalizing-fermats-last-theorem) · [Anthropic (Fable 5.2)](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-fable-and-mythos-5-2) · [Kevin Buzzard](https://xenaproject.wordpress.com/2026/09/04/flt-anthropic-has-beaten-me-to-it/)</small>
- **Classification of finite simple groups.** Axiom's agent swarms, directed by its team, formalized 'the enormous theorem' in 24 days, producing ~43.1M lines of Lean. The human proof spans ~15,000 pages in 500+ papers by ~100 mathematicians. *(Axiom's own claim, with review so far self-assessed; it built on ~1.2M lines of earlier Lean work. For comparison, the Feit–Thompson step alone took a human team 6 years in Coq (2012).)*<br><small>⏱ 24 days · 👥 Axiom team + dozens of agents · [Axiom](https://axiommath.ai/research/the-enormous-theorem-formalized/) · [Lean code](https://github.com/AxiomMath/CFSG)</small>
- **Resolution of singularities.** Agents formalized Hironaka's 1964 theorem in about a week (Sep 4–10, 2026): ~600K lines of Lean for about $135K in tokens. *(Resolution's own figures, and the cost is its estimate. Two more leads then spent two weeks refactoring it. Review is self-assessed.)*<br><small>⏱ ~1 week · 👥 1 mathematician + agents · [Resolution](https://resolution.org/post/formalizing-resolution-of-singularities) · [Lean code](https://github.com/resolutionorg/hironaka)</small>
- **Exponential Time Hypothesis, disproved.** OpenAI's system found a disproof on Oct 16, 2026, and it was formalized in Lean the next day. *(Lab claim. Impagliazzo, Paturi and Williams are running an independent review, which is not yet a certification.)*<br><small>⏱ 1 day to Lean · 👥 AI system · [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/) · [UC San Diego](https://today.ucsd.edu/story/computer-scientists-facilitating-an-independent-review-of-a-high-profile-open-ai-and-anthropic-resul)</small>
- **Local smoothing → Kakeya.** OpenAI's system proved the local smoothing conjecture in all dimensions on Oct 18, 2026 (which implies Kakeya), and it was formalized in Lean the next day. *(Lab claim.)*<br><small>⏱ 1 day to Lean · 👥 AI system · [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)</small>
- **722 manuscripts.** One OpenAI model was posed ~4,000 problems and produced 722 manuscripts in 374 result families (count as of Nov 23, 2026). OpenAI estimates the average result took compute equal to about 3 hours of ChatGPT Pro thinking. *(About 42% are formalized. In October OpenAI withdrew 3 manuscripts over a sign error and revised 14 others.)*<br><small>⏱ ~3 Pro-hours of compute per result · 👥 1 model, some human editing · [OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/) · [GitHub](https://github.com/openai/math) · [Change history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md)</small>
- **Aaronson–Ambainis conjecture.** Open since 2008. A Physical Superintelligence system, running on a non-public Anthropic model, had a complete proof, accepted by Lean, on day 3 of its run. *(PSI's own account. A PSI researcher scoped the problem, ran the system and reviewed the result.)*<br><small>⏱ 3 days · 👥 1 researcher + AI system · [PSI](https://psi.inc/research/aaronson-ambainis/) · [Paper (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09773)</small>
- **Cycle double cover conjecture.** Open for about 50 years. GPT-5.6 Sol Ultra, running up to 64 sub-agents from a single prompt, proved it, and the proof is kernel-checked in Lean. *(OpenAI's Knight says the run took just under an hour, though the prompt asks for at least eight. The run time has not been checked independently.)*<br><small>⏱ &lt;1 h (OpenAI's figure) · 👥 1 model, up to 64 sub-agents · [Prompt (OpenAI)](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/04d1d1e4-bc75-476a-97cf-49055cd98d31/cdc_prompt.pdf) · [Proof (OpenAI)](https://cdn.openai.com/pdf/04d1d1e4-bc75-476a-97cf-49055cd98d31/cdc_proof.pdf) · [Lean proof](https://github.com/openai/cdc-lean) · [Run time (Knight on X)](https://x.com/__eknight__/status/2075643450196971805) · [Exposition (Oum)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.16356)</small>
- **Dinitz–Garg–Goemans conjecture.** Dmitry Rybin found a 7-node counterexample to the ~30-year-old cost conjecture in a 5.5-hour session with GPT-5.6 Pro, using four prompts totalling under 60 words. *(Rybin says he had thought about the problem for weeks beforehand, and the paper was attached to the chat. The counterexample has since been checked in Isabelle.)*<br><small>⏱ 5.5 h session · 👥 1 person + 1 model · [Rybin on X](https://x.com/DmitryRybin1/status/2079904005652893709) · [Rybin (follow-up)](https://x.com/DmitryRybin1/status/2079906232069177643) · [New Scientist](https://www.newscientist.com/article/2580932-extremely-basic-ai-prompt-cracks-decades-old-maths-problem/) · [Isabelle check](https://isa-afp.org/entries/Dinitz_Garg_Goemans_Counterexample.html)</small>
- **Sphere packing in 24 dimensions.** Math Inc's Gauss formalized the 24-dimension proof (Cohn, Kumar, Miller, Radchenko and Viazovska) in two weeks, working from the paper and the existing Lean codebase. It had earlier finished the 8-dimension case in 5 days, where the human team had estimated 6 more months. *(Math Inc's own account.)*<br><small>⏱ 2 weeks · 👥 Agent, building on earlier human Lean work · [Math Inc](https://www.math.inc/sphere-packing) · [Lean code](https://github.com/math-inc/Sphere-Packing-Lean)</small>
- **Strong prime number theorem.** Gauss finished Tao and Kontorovich's formalization challenge in 3 weeks, with human-written scaffolding and expert guidance. After about 18 months the human project had finished the medium-strength version, parts of which Gauss reused.<br><small>⏱ 3 weeks · 👥 Agent + human experts · [Math Inc](https://www.math.inc/gauss) · [Lean code](https://github.com/math-inc/strongpnt)</small>
- **4×4 matrix multiplication.** AlphaEvolve found a way to multiply 4×4 complex matrices with 48 multiplications instead of 49. It was the first improvement over Strassen's 1969 method in this setting in 56 years.<br><small>⏱ — · 👥 DeepMind + agent · [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaevolve-a-gemini-powered-coding-agent-for-designing-advanced-algorithms/)</small>
- **IMO gold in plain English.** An advanced Gemini Deep Think scored 35/42 at IMO 2025 within the contest's two 4.5-hour sessions, working from the official problems. IMO coordinators graded its solutions at gold-medal standard. *(The IMO graded the answers. It did not validate the system or how it was run.)*<br><small>⏱ 2 × 4.5 h · 👥 No humans during exam (DeepMind's account) · [Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/advanced-version-of-gemini-with-deep-think-officially-achieves-gold-medal-standard-at-the-international-mathematical-olympiad/) · [IMO statement](https://imo2025.au/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/IMO-2025_ClosingDayStatement-19072025.pdf)</small>

### Science &amp; bio

- **Scorpion-toxin binders.** In 64 hours Claude generated 318,484 protein designs and picked 180. Outside labs later tested 211 designs (31 added by humans), and 17 met the binding bar, each against one of four toxins. *(Anthropic's own report. Binding only; neutralizing the toxins has not been shown.)*<br><small>⏱ 64 h of design · 👥 Model + a few nudges; 2 outside labs for testing · [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/claude-scorpion-toxin-binder-design)</small>
- **Superbug hypothesis in 48 hours.** Google's AI co-scientist independently reached Imperial's unpublished, years-in-the-making hypothesis about how cf-PICIs jump between bacterial species. *(Imperial had already proved it in the lab. The AI matched the hypothesis; it did not do the experiments.)*<br><small>⏱ ~2 days · 👥 AI co-scientist + Imperial researchers · [BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/clyz6e9edy3o) · [Google Research](https://research.google/blog/accelerating-scientific-breakthroughs-with-an-ai-co-scientist/) · [Imperial](https://www.imperial.ac.uk/news/261293/googles-ai-co-scientist-could-enhance-research/)</small>
- **Robin finds a dry-AMD candidate.** FutureHouse's multi-agent Robin proposed ripasudil for dry macular degeneration and went from concept to submitted paper in 2.5 months. Humans wrote the protocols and ran the wet-lab work. *(The evidence so far is from cell assays, not patients.)*<br><small>⏱ 2.5 months · 👥 Agents + lab team · [FutureHouse](https://www.futurehouse.org/research/demonstrating-end-to-end-scientific-discovery-with-robin-a-multi-agent-system) · [Nature (2026)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10652-y)</small>
- **First AI-written genomes.** Arc's Evo models wrote whole bacteriophage genomes, using the phage ΦX174 as a template. Of 285 tested, 16 were viable phages that infected and lysed lab strains of E. coli. *('First' is Arc's own claim.)*<br><small>⏱ — · 👥 Arc / Stanford team · [Arc Institute](https://arcinstitute.org/news/hie-king-first-synthetic-phage)</small>
- **Rentosertib.** Insilico went from a new AI-picked fibrosis target to a preclinical candidate in ~18 months on a company-reported budget of ~$2.6M. Phase 2a results followed in Nature Medicine (2025). *(The phase 2a trial was small: 71 patients over 12 weeks, with safety as the main endpoint.)*<br><small>⏱ ~18 months · 👥 Insilico + generative AI · [Nature Biotech (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11738990/) · [Nature Medicine (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12353801/) · [Insilico (cost)](https://insilico.com/phase1)</small>
- **Mithril Medicine.** A company with one full-time founder used Claude to coordinate consultants and 11 outside labs, and took a muscular-dystrophy treatment from idea to promising mouse results in under 18 months. *(Anthropic's account. The mouse results are an unpublished pilot.)*<br><small>⏱ &lt;18 months · 👥 1 founder + consultants + 11 labs · [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/early-progress-drug-development)</small>
- **AlphaFold DB, 200M structures.** One July 2022 update expanded the AlphaFold database from ~1M to 200M+ predicted structures, nearly every catalogued protein. *(These are predictions, not experimental structures.)*<br><small>⏱ One release · 👥 DeepMind + EMBL-EBI · [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphafold-reveals-the-structure-of-the-protein-universe/)</small>
- **Halicin.** MIT's model picked halicin, a structurally new antibiotic that worked in mice, out of a library of about 6,000 compounds. A separate screen of 107M molecules then took 4 days and turned up 23 more candidates. *(Halicin did not come from the big screen. MIT News says that screen took three days; the Cell paper says four.)*<br><small>⏱ 4 days for the 107M screen · 👥 MIT team · [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2020/artificial-intelligence-identifies-new-antibiotic-0220) · [Cell paper (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8349178/)</small>
- **10-day forecast in a minute.** GraphCast produces a global 10-day weather forecast in under 60 seconds on one TPU.<br><small>⏱ &lt;1 min · 👥 Model · [DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/graphcast-ai-model-for-faster-and-more-accurate-global-weather-forecasting/)</small>

### Materials &amp; hardware

One torch test; the alloy is not yet scaled up or qualified.)*<br><small>⏱ 16 weeks · 👥 Autonomous lab + Purdue testers · [Radical AI](https://www.radical-ai.com/news/purdue-applied-research-institute-confirms-superior-performance-of-radical-ai-materials)</small>

### Security

- **Signal, formally verified.** Google DeepMind's AlphaProof Code agents verified the core of Signal's cryptographic code in four weeks, writing 6M+ lines of Lean. *(DeepMind's own claim. - **Firefox.** In two weeks Claude found 22 security bugs in Firefox, 14 of them high severity, and the first came after 20 minutes. - **RSA-260.** Cognition engineer Eric Lu, working with Devin, went from first prompt to the factors of RSA-260 in about three weeks, using an estimated ~4,900 GPU-days. *(An RSA Challenge number from 1991, and the largest factored so far. - **OpenSSL zero-days.** AISLE's AI found all 12 CVEs in OpenSSL's January 2026 release and 18 of the 24 fixed in curl 8.23.0. *(Vendor-reported; the counts match the maintainers' advisories.
