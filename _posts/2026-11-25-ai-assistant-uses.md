A starter list for anyone beginning with Grok Bot, Claude, ChatGPT, or another AI assistant.

Sourced from [Tim Ferriss's Nov 22, 2026 thread](https://x.com/tferriss/status/2124313547773771817) asking: *“What's the most useful thing you've done with AI assistants/agents so far, even if mundane?”* Tim's own example: in about 48 hours, he found and cancelled old subscriptions across multiple cards, leftover insurance policies, and outdated employee services — about **$10,000 a year** saved.

**Read ~450 unique posts** in the conversation (the original had 321 replies, plus nested replies and quote posts). About 380 had enough substance to review. Collected Nov 25, 2026.

---

### TL;DR — the top 10

These came up most often, or were the stories people kept pointing to.

1. **Audit and cancel forgotten subscriptions and leftover policies.** The most repeated use in the thread, and Tim's own example (~$10k/year).
2. **Work an insurance claim or shop a better policy.** A Palisades fire playbook was the most-liked reply. Others cut premiums or found a better mortgage ($13k/year).
3. **Catch tax credits, messy IRS issues, and expense reimbursements.** One person found a niche credit their CPA missed and amended for a $30k adjustment.
4. **Chase refunds the company hoped you'd drop** — flights, hotels, charge disputes, a YouTube subscription on the wrong account.
5. **Run the family calendar** from school emails, PDFs, and photos, including events you'd otherwise miss.
6. **Plan meals around the week's calendar and fill the grocery cart**, including preferred brands and digital coupons.
7. **Plan a trip, then handle the ugly parts** — itineraries, airline holds, delay compensation, a cheaper rebooking.
8. **Build a small app or tool for yourself**, even if you've never written code (anxiety app, language app, glucose watch face, meal planner).
9. **Clean the inbox and surface only what matters** — spam filters, school Canvas messages, old email archives, data-broker opt-outs.
10. **Draft, proof, and edit** — emails, homework, video, podcasts, even a letter from your future self.

The pattern people kept repeating: the boring, recurring jobs (audits, calendars, claims, carts) beat the flashy demos. Several also said the same safety rule: let the assistant find and draft; you approve anything that spends money or cancels an account.

### Money

The most crowded category in the thread.

- **Audit every recurring charge.** Give the assistant read access to statements or card exports and ask it to list every subscription, leftover insurance policy, and service you no longer use. Tim did this across multiple cards in 48 hours (~$10,000/year). Many others said they found at least one charge they'd forgotten.
  - *Try:* “Here are three months of statements. List every recurring payment, what it's for, when it last provided value, and which ones look safe to cancel. Don't cancel anything — just make a ranked list with evidence.”
- **Cancel the ones that only take a phone call.** Have it call, sit on hold, and send back the transcript.
- **Shop a cheaper mortgage, with a script for the call.** One person had Muse find a better mortgage and walk through exactly who to call and what to say. Saving $13k a year, $91,000 over seven years.
- **Haggle an insurance renewal.** One person's agent anchored on last year's price, pulled market data, demanded alternatives, and followed up on the manager's discount. Same coverage, R$4,085 → R$2,710 (–34%). He just tapped “close.”
- **Fight an insurance claim.** After losing a home in the Palisades fire, one person used Grok as a claims team: read every letter like a lawyer, upload the policy, audit the lowball rebuild estimate against current labor and material costs, build a thousands-line contents inventory, draft firm emails with a deadline, and look up the insurer's legal response deadlines. He says it levels the field; it does not replace a lawyer on a big claim, and you should verify what it tells you.
  - *Try:* “Act as a policyholder attorney in [state]. Find errors, deadlines, vague language, and anything I should push back on. Cite my policy and state law.”
  - *Also try:* “What are my limits, exclusions, and deadlines? What am I owed that I haven't asked for?” and “What's typically part of a claim like mine that I haven't listed?”
- **Catch a leftover vehicle still on your insurance** after a trade-in.
- **Find a tax credit your accountant missed.** One person built a project to audit taxes, found a niche credit the CPA missed, got a script for how to explain it, and amended for a $30k return adjustment, three days before it expired. Another saved about $5k doing taxes. A third dropped a draft 2025 filing and the accountant's documents into Claude and got a four-page report that included items the accountant missed.
- **Handle an IRS mix-up.** One person's Grok Bot created petitions, turned photos into PDFs, and explained the situation in words the IRS would understand. Another used it for tax returns and admin going years back.
- **Track investments and tax-loss harvesting.** One person built a local dashboard for fractional shares across brokerages and drops in statements each month.
- **Match health expenses** across EOBs, FSA payments, and card transactions. One person built an agent plus an iPhone app to submit HRA copays, bills, and premiums (receipt, category, and dates) on a clunky benefits site.
- **Get expense reimbursements you'd otherwise miss.** One person used ChatGPT Finance and found a hotel had charged a personal card instead of a corporate one — almost $2k that wouldn't have been expensed.
- **Automate an expense tracker.** One person called this the biggest time saver; the biggest ROI was replacing expensive SaaS with tools they built themselves.
- **Price-adjust a purchase after it goes on sale.** One person had Muse watch a Christmas tree for 30 days, got a $150 reduction notice, and went in-store for the adjustment when the agent couldn't get it on the phone.
- **Dispute a charge, pay a parking ticket, or handle a passport application.** One person won a parking ticket, and also handled winter car-storage insurance and a baby crib for a hotel. Another also won a parking ticket.
- **Sell a car and compare offers** from the big retailers, then pick the best.
- **Review a phone bill and get a script for the call.** One person had it analyze a Verizon bill, saved $100/month, and got a script for exactly what to say and ask for.

### Inbox, calendar, and everyday admin

- **Triage a noisy inbox.** A college student has an assistant read school email and Canvas, ignore the spam, and ping only the time-sensitive messages. Another person runs an hourly spam-scan that used to break him to clear by hand. A third uses Copilot to actually find emails in Outlook.
- **Clean out old inboxes and shut them down.** One person found buried property records in Gmail, renamed and filed them by property, deleted thousands of old emails, and set filters for new ones (~4 hours over a few days). Another cleaned 120,000 emails from two old addresses and built a checklist to move anything useful before closing them.
- **Put prescriptions on the calendar**, with a different reminder song by time of day and by pill. One person wrote a prompt their mom could give her own assistant.
- **Build the family calendar from whatever you've got** — websites, PDFs, photos. One parent: two kids' school calendars, scouting, soccer, art classes, doctor appointments; 60+ events scheduled in the first week of September. A single dad of two: calendar around school days, school events caught from email, cheap weekend outings, plus a planned Thanksgiving week and Black Friday list.
- **Call your phone instead of sending another ignored notification** when it's time to start a task. One person built Emergency Ring so a trusted person — or the agent, under rules — can ring an iPhone even on silent and Focus.
- **Turn messy spreadsheets, receipts, and exports into a checklist** instead of losing a Sunday to admin. One person turns purchase-order PDFs into a usable Excel format.
- **Organize thousands of photos** into folders by date and subject.
- **Clean a slow computer** (old temp files, duplicates, apps that launch themselves) — and tell it not to delete anything without permission.
- **Sort the desktop / files.**
- **Ask it to remove your info from data-broker sites.**
- **Track firmware and software updates** for routers, servers, and cameras. One person loaded devices from a photo of the model/serial sticker and has a routine that compares current versions to new releases.
- **Find a real phone number** when a company's site only offers a chatbot loop.
- **Keep a “reread this series before the sequel drops” reminder** that moves if the release date slips.
- **Ping you when your partner's car pulls into the driveway** so you can go help unload.
- **Log library books, get ratings, and order the next batch** so you don't reread the same toddler books (SF public library).

### Family and home

- **Plan the week's meals around the actual calendar**, then build the grocery list and fill the cart. One end-to-end version reads the family calendar, plans 15-minute or make-ahead meals on busy nights, avoids what the kids are sick of and what a spouse can't eat, and cuts planning-plus-clicking down to about 15 minutes. Another person built a meal-planner app with recipe cards, pantry tracking, and a grocery list in one evening.
- **Add groceries in plain language**, using preferred brands from past orders, without clicking through every screen. For new items, one version searches the store catalog, you confirm once, and it remembers. Another logs into grocery accounts and clips digital coupons.
- **Weekly activity sheet for the kids, plus a weekend activity finder** that accounts for weather, travel, and ticket prices and drops it on the calendar.
- **A talking interactive map for a four-year-old.**
- **Real-time swim-meet stats** for parents in the stands.
- **A daily outfit**, from photos of your closet, based on the weather and the calendar.
- **Interior design from photos, a Pinterest board, and a floor plan** — measure furniture, source pieces within a budget, and build a project plan. One person did this with Astra.
- **Home maintenance:** upload what's in the house, create RFQs, and have it contact and schedule local service companies from its own Gmail.
- **Figure out an unfamiliar appliance.** One person used it to learn how various Miele stovetops work at different Airbnbs. Another used it to diagnose a kitchen mixer tap, find the part, and get replacement steps.
- **Ask what a dishwasher cycle actually costs** at local water and electricity rates. One person found it was 17 cents and stopped hand-washing.
- **Control smart lights after the company dies**, or see what's on in your city tonight. One person built an NYC event aggregator and a local app for lights.
- **A bedtime suggestion** that depends on how long the kid napped at daycare.
- **Suggest general contractors** from public permits, then draft intros to 2nd-degree LinkedIn connections.

### Health and fitness

- **Sit on hold with doctors' offices and book in-network appointments.** August's agent spent 24,286 minutes on hold for early users; 366 holds for one person.
- **A weekly exercise plan that fits the real calendar** — PT, gymnastics, travel, a long bike ride, soccer drills — then adjusts after you review the week.
- **Coach a training block.** One person trained for a sprint triathlon with an AI coach for 12 weeks; it predicted their time within 6 seconds, and they finished 5th of 95 in the age group.
- **Sync a gym app and body scans**, notice skipped workouts, schedule the makeup, and put it on the calendar.
- **A bigger, clearer glucose readout on a smartwatch**, built first for personal use, then shared (ClearBG Watch; diabetes and vision challenges).
- **Stay current on medical literature** with a daily new-papers summary.
- **Match what you actually spend on care** to what was covered (see [Money](#money): EOBs / FSA / HRA).
- **Run a careful n-of-1 on supplements** — track health data, compare placebo vs. real, and adjust doses on what actually moves the needle (this is a personal experiment one person described; not medical advice).
- **An anti-anxiety app**, built without having written a line of code.
- **Draft clinic paperwork for a human to check.** Patients fill in info before the visit; AI drafts reports and certificates for the clinician to review.

### Travel

- **Build a real itinerary.** One person got a two-week Ireland plan (restaurants, pubs, tours, walking distances, booking windows, bus routes, history) in minutes after a long prompt. Another used it as a hyper-local tour guide for a birthday weekend in a new city.
- **Plan an unusual trip end to end.** One couple is cycling Japan; the assistant builds Ride with GPS routes, researches camping, hot springs, and local festivals, tracks progress, and makes daily audio guides.
- **Find a place to live, book the viewing, then furnish it.** One person gave it criteria, showed up at an address and time, then handed over the floor plan and had it buy fitted furniture to taste.
- **Watch a booked hotel and rebook if the price drops.** One person rebooked at almost $200 less per night.
- **Get an airline refund or delay payout you'd given up on.** One person found an old Air Canada cancellation text, read the regulations, filed the form, and got a $1k refund two days later. Another went after avoidable delays over nine hours; after a first “no,” the bot's emails got $2,000 for an adult and a lap child. A third's American Airlines refund ($1,131.60) landed on a Chase Travel virtual card they didn't know existed; the agent found the emails, matched ticket numbers, called Chase, and handed over the call. A fourth no longer waits on hold or chat for airline issues.
- **Get a refund when you signed up on the wrong account.** One person had Muse reach YouTube support (the contact page was nearly impossible to find) and recovered $8.99.
- **Book when the airline's own site keeps rejecting the card.** One person got a working Stripe link for Air India Express after the airline site failed with the same card.
- **Translate “how this stove works” in a short-term rental** (see [Family and home](#family-and-home)).

### Learning and kids

- **Personalized math homework from a photo of what came home from school.** One parent built a Grok Bot for a 2nd grader who struggles with math facts, following Math Academy principles, California standards, and research on dyscalculia. It weaves in the fact he's working on, tracks every fact on a dashboard, and — after the teacher saw the work — replaced the class homework. The tutor asked how to set it up for other students. Another parent makes practice quizzes from a 3rd grader's study guides.
- **A language app that drills vocab, conjugations, and grammar, and checks writing and speaking.** One person built it while studying a language. Another couldn't find a word-a-day app that taught te reo Māori the way they wanted, so they made one. A third built a Spanish-learning app alongside spending, journal, and calendar apps.
- **A study agent for going back to school** — what to study today, and a schedule, so the time goes into studying, not planning.
- **Turn 15 years of bookmarks and 20 years of ebooks and course PDFs into a searchable second-brain wiki**, then ask it questions or have it write a tutorial from your own archive.
- **Sketchnotes of learning materials** so the important parts are visible at a glance.
- **Videos on the lives of the Saints**, made with a daughter and controlled over Signal.
- **Use university course outlines to build a study schedule.**

### Research and staying current

- **A morning one-pager** of only the news, markets, your teams' scores, and weather that matters, including a surface map.
- **A personal weekly culture digest** — new TV, cinema, theatre, exhibitions, podcasts, YouTube — based on your email and Goodreads, then logged to a database (a personal version of Tim's 5-Bullet Friday).
- **A weekly “dump what I didn't have time to read, get it back as a clean pack.”**
- **Turn a three-hour podcast into one paragraph**, or a full episode into a same-day written summary and post.
- **A research-to-podcast pipeline:** papers in, novel takes, long-form audio out.
- **A daily digest of your own site and YouTube analytics**, with a weekly “what's going wrong and how to reverse it” when the trend is off.
- **Competitive or local research.** One person gets a weekly list of fellowships and internships. Another drops a Search Console export and asks which pages kept impressions but lost clicks — that's the rewrite list. A local wedding magazine gets a daily report on what every advertiser posted, plus big bridal brands and celebrity weddings.
- **Pull a transcript before you share a viral clip** — the caption and the real quote often don't match.
- **Look up a pin-out and a test procedure** for a car circuit board you wouldn't have figured out alone.

### Writing, conversations, and creative work

- **Proofread for the tiny mistake humans miss.** In one case, Grok found a phone-number discrepancy no one had caught for years.
- **A second pair of eyes on your own draft** — weak argument, missing evidence, the sentence a reader would trip on.
- **Prep a hard conversation.** One person used it to organize thoughts, outline the talk, and suggest where to ask specific questions. It went well.
- **Rewrite a legal document into plain English** and fix the family tree, then send a side-by-side back to the lawyers (an inheritance matter in Trinidad). Another person also uses it to compile case information and keep lawyers on track.
- **Write an obituary** from the facts you feed it. One person also used it to time inclusions in a complicated panettone bake.
- **A letter from yourself 20 years in the future**, read aloud at dinner. One person called it one of the most emotional family moments they've had.
- **Draft replies in your voice** — Google reviews the same day; X posts every couple of hours from what's moving in your niche, then you edit and post. One X-draft agent checks sent mail first so its owner doesn't reply to someone they already cold-emailed that morning.
- **Edit video at volume.** One person connects Claude to DaVinci Resolve Studio via MCP, with their own templates and design system: 20–30 full-length YouTube videos and 60 shorts in a week, or a 20-minute video with motion graphics in hours (a few revisions, then ship). They estimated this would have cost ~$10k before. Another trained a model on their editing style and removed the non-creative busywork.
- **Podcast publishing busywork** — YouTube to Spotify/RSS, including video.
- **An Instagram account of words**, plus bots for fitness, a move to Mexico, and project management, inspired by a 5-Bullet Friday.
- **A book club, from idea to waitlist in six hours.**

### Coding and building your own tools

You do not have to be a developer. Several of the best stories in the thread start with “I told it what I wanted in plain English.”

- **Build the small app you've been wishing existed.** Anti-anxiety app, Goodie Jar for little wins (inspired by Tim's *Tools of Titans*), a te reo Māori word-a-day app, a language tutor, a glucose watch face, a meal planner, a neighborhood map, a free well-site locator for oil-and-gas field work, an interactive community map of 140 sites, a contact directory that refreshes itself (~8k visitors/month), a city debt clock.
- **A neighborhood map Google doesn't have** — local makers, parks and trails, what's being built. One person built it for fun; more than 2,000 neighbors use it every month. A follow-up: it was built in Three.js with real elevation and OpenStreetMap data, and now they add useful listings (for example, elementary-school opening forms) and share them.
- **Generate CAD / a STEP file from dimensions**, instead of doing it by hand.
- **“Find all implicit state machines in my codebase”** — one reply, quoting Tobi, called this the only prompt you need to improve a codebase.
- **A read-only review agent** that reads pull requests, test results, and incident notes, then gives you risks and open questions before you start.
- **Let synthetic users poke at a product** before real humans do, to find the boring confusing bits.
- **Hire in another language, fast.** One person used Claude Code, Gmail, ElevenLabs, and a rented Indian number: a newspaper ad, a Hindi-speaking phone agent that screens applicants, and a driver hired on day 3.

### Work and business

This thread's audience is more entrepreneurial than average, so these showed up a lot. Still useful if you just want to steal the pattern: invoices, follow-ups, and documents that otherwise die in an inbox.

- **Find better prices on the things you already buy.** One person (BAMBOO / Healthy Foods) built a tool on OpenAI Codex that reads 12 months of restaurant invoices, connects to QuickBooks and vendors, tracks prices daily, and searches for equivalent suppliers. Savings cited: about $18k/year on broccoli, $7k on olive oil, $25k on packaging. A follow-up describes a Greek olive-oil importer worth buying a month's supply from to hit the minimum order.
- **A competitive price benchmark** from years of trade data, on about $2 million a year of product purchases.
- **A CRM you didn't buy.** One person (B2B to European municipalities): every email synced to Notion, a follow-up queue for cold leads, news alerts, and outreach drafts in four languages — review and hit send. Another automated a CRM and connected it to Notion in 20 minutes. A third runs a lead agent per company on one shared database and reads each morning what they did and why.
- **Collapse a brutal internal form.** One person took 10 report types, each with five levels of drop-downs, and boiled them down to three required inputs.
- **Turn meetings into tickets and docs automatically.** An agent joins the call, drafts governance/delivery docs, and puts tickets in Jira — the write-up had been eating the week.
- **Turn a call into a list of decisions, owners, and open questions** instead of replaying the whole thing. Another person scores every leasing call against the same rubric the minute it ends.
- **Draft service agreements and invoices.** ~30 minutes saved per run.
- **Chase the documents and the one contract clause that matters** so a real-estate deal doesn't die in someone's inbox.
- **A free, weekly-updated database of commercial real estate sales and leases**, run by Grok Bots that scrape announcements, build pages, and QA them.
- **Office staff for a small business**, enough to avoid a hire. One person fully automated accounts payable; Claude estimated ~500 hours a year. Another vibe-coded the audit process for 100+ bakeries and cut wastage from above 5% to below 2%.
- **Hospital insurance-desk work:** read documents, fill pre-authorization forms, file them on each insurer's portal, and track the claim (Meion Labs, India).
- **Daily outreach, lead follow-up, and quoting** with light supervision. One person described this running the business day to day (their “generated 10k” line was cut off in the post we captured, so the exact result isn't clear).
- **A $2 million/month Meta ads budget**, managed with Grok Bots.
- **Replace a payment stack.** One person rebuilt outdated payment software and moved a title company to Stripe, saving $3,000/year with ~$100/year upkeep.
- **An “office of one” for hiring, sales, and public posts**, with separate agents that don't step on each other.
- **Cancel a fundraising round you thought you needed.** One person said Grok Bot made it possible to cancel a £2m raise — and told Tim to give the bot the biggest problem, not just the discount-finding.

### A few obvious extras (not from the thread)

These didn't show up as concrete stories in Tim's thread, but they're reasonable first asks if you're just getting started and nothing above has clicked yet:

- **Summarize a long PDF, contract, or article**, then list the open questions.
- **Compare two products, plans, or schools** side by side, with sources.
- **Help you pack for a trip** from the calendar and the weather.
- **Role-play a job interview or a vendor call**, then give notes.
- **Explain a lab result or a school letter** in plain language — then tell you what to ask a professional, not what to decide on your own.

### How to use this list

Pick one line, paste it into Grok Bot (or whatever you use), and attach the relevant files or connect the relevant account. Start with **read-only** access when money or email is involved. Several people in the thread said the same thing: have the assistant find, list, and draft; you approve anything that cancels, pays, or sends.

The jobs that came up most often were not “be my CEO.” They were audits, calendars, claims, carts, refunds, and homework. That's a good place to start.
